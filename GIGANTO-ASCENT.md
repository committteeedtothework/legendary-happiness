## Implemented

New encounters use Giganto's visible armor for the Emerald ascent. The contact surface is extracted from 42,646 triangles of the supplied GLB, indexed in a spatial grid. Movement projects onto nearby triangles and parallel-transports its direction across changing normals. It does not follow an invisible route or collide with floating platforms. If sustained uphill movement stalls at a sharp lip, short lateral steps seek another real armor contact; manual lateral input takes priority. Small golden guides mark actual armor contacts. The quiet ascent stance and contact surface share the same moving/turning root; the existing fighting rig takes over after transformation.

W/S ascend/descend, A/D traverse sideways, Shift boosts, Space jumps off, and E releases or grips within reach. Running into an accessible foot grips automatically. Mounting blends over 0.14 seconds. Running uses 15 m/s, boosting 25 m/s and 20 boost energy/s before the existing regeneration. Camera clearance traces actual armor triangles and seeks a clear neighboring view. Dorsal pulses warn and eject exposed climbers; boosting can resist at an energy cost. Twelve earned height checkpoints award five rings each once. Falls return to an earned armor checkpoint. Saved anchors are reprojected onto valid geometry; corrupt anchors fail safely. The final Emerald remains required before Super Sonic and combat.

Wall-run and wall-idle Sonic clips plus a fixed traversal stance for Giganto bring runtime libraries to **31 Sonic / 30 Giganto** clips. GLBs retain 22 / 24 clips. No mesh, texture, joint length or bind scale was changed. Existing in-progress intro/traversal saves without the new setting keep the original ruins route. **Battle Settings → Combat → Ascent** can select Armor or Ruins; choose before starting a new trial.

**Battle Settings → Graphics → Contact shading** offers Off, Low and High. The arena uses non-temporal screen-space ambient occlusion, 16 samples at half resolution or 32 at three-quarter resolution, with restrained strength and blur. It requires post-processing, Balanced/High quality and float-color rendering support. Sky, beams, glow, particles and moving alpha foliage are excluded. A simple ground shadow remains the low-quality/unsupported fallback. Dynamic directional shadows remain independently configurable. Pass resources are resized/disposed with the arena, and shared renderer/visibility state is restored even after an exception. Overworld settings and rendering remain isolated.

## Remaining limitations

This is a staged custom interpretation, not the complete official Sonic Frontiers encounter. Armor climbing uses the exact mesh in a deliberately fixed joint stance plus moving/turning root; it does **not** support arbitrary independently deforming limbs during climbing. Contact is a simplified player capsule/sphere, with small seam tolerance. The camera can still be tight near complex armor. The route selector is locked during an active encounter; choose a different route before starting a new trial.

Screen-space contact shading sees only on-screen geometry; it is not cascaded or ray-traced shadowing. Low/High settings incur extra normal/depth and full-screen passes. Directional shadows, radial boost blur, original terrain, supplied model materials, facial/finger rigging, official full ability/choreography roster and exact commercial cutscene/music timing retain the limitations documented in previous reports. No new official assets, recordings, paid generation or external reference lookup were used. Supplied asset/license notes remain in TITAN-ASSETS.md and THIRD_PARTY.md.
