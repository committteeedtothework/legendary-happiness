# Giganto music direction — 2.8.15

This update changes Giganto soundtrack selection, cue configuration and cinematic clock alignment. The 2.8.14 arena presentation, combat balance, movement, models, portal objective, rewards, overworld gameplay/audio and existing world-save envelope are retained.

## Reference and assets

Reference scope is the original Sonic Frontiers (2022) Kronos/Giganto encounter. The [Sirus Gaming encounter guide](https://sirusgaming.com/sonic-frontiers-giganto-boss-guide/) was read directly and confirms preparation before ascent, the final Emerald, Super Sonic aerial combat, the half-health dorsal-emitter reveal, beam/grab counters and the finishing attack. Its [linked YTSunny fight footage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=M2XRHLJMGVQ) was inaccessible: the environment's HTTP proxy returned 403. No proxy bypass was attempted. Frame-by-frame cinematic and lyrical timestamps could therefore **not** be independently verified in this update. Machine-readable reference/provenance notes are in reference/giganto-music-2.8.15.json.

The complete existing user-supplied **Titan: GIGANTO** and **Undefeatable** MP3s remain unchanged. They are user-provided soundtrack recordings, not independently licensed DCSMCRAFT assets; their rights notices remain. No additional commercial audio was downloaded or claimed as licensed. Original game stems, isolated transitions/stingers, an official event/beat map and original cinematic sound effects are unavailable. Combat effects remain the existing original Foley/synthesis. This is a custom adaptive arrangement, with the source-local cue defaults documented in GIGANTO-MUSIC.md; it does not claim identical Frontiers timing.

## Working music direction

- Prelude: the supplied Titan recording loops during portal introduction, ring preparation, climb and transformation. Its actual native-media cursor now survives world save/reload, including delayed metadata. Moving into combat releases the prelude before the battle recording plays.
- Battle opening: the transformation-to-combat event selects the opening of the battle track. Actual battle state, rather than elapsed overworld time, owns the handoff. A skipped transformation goes directly to the combat cue.
- First phase: the opening flows into the configured loop; rendering hitches do not repeatedly seek or restart it. Damage and ring drain determine phase duration. No extra invulnerability or forced combat wait is added to fill a song.
- Half health: the same stage event that starts the emitter cinematic selects a four-second pickup leading to the configured phase-two downbeat. A natural transition keeps the same audio voice when its cursor has entered the selected chorus, including late rendered frames; a skipped transition selects the phase-two cue immediately.
- Finale: the finishing event selects the seven-second closing passage. Charge, launch, contact and rupture remain consumed simulation events, with cue IDs preventing replay after pause/reload. Cinematic pose clocks follow the actual audio cursor with the pending simulation steps subtracted, fixing the old double advancement that could put poses ahead of the song. Endpoint comparisons tolerate numeric rounding so a pose just below 4/7 seconds cannot hold the section open. Retry detection uses the explicit attempt counter rather than small pose-clock corrections. No wall-clock timeout schedules a phase, contact, music section or victory.
- Retry, defeat and exit select/stop their sections through encounter state. Sources are bounded, gains ramp, late decode results are discarded after disposal, and music/voice resources are released on return. Muted/unavailable music falls back to the normal simulation clock without blocking play. Device/output latency and dropped frames still limit perceived synchronization; sample-perfect speaker-to-screen timing is not claimed.

Entrance/transformation shots and individual attack counters retain their authored clocks and event-driven Foley; only the half-health reveal and finale use the battle recording as their pose clock. Their complete official lyrical/choreographic timing is not reproduced. The reference guide establishes encounter structure, not the default source cue timestamps.

## Local tracks and cues

Inside the arena, open **Escape → Battle settings → Audio → Soundtrack**. Choose a local MP3 (up to 16 MiB and 15 minutes). Native metadata is checked before full PCM allocation; decode and required cue ranges are validated before selection. Decoded PCM is reused for the imported playback copy. Invalid/short files leave the current selection intact. Imported files stay in this PC/browser's local music store; they are never sent through multiplayer or placed in world backups. A world backup carries only the track ID/name/duration and cue map. If the local file is missing on another PC/browser, playback reports that error rather than silently choosing different music. Re-select the file or choose **Use supplied track**. If persistent storage is unavailable, the import is explicitly marked session-only.

**Cue times · seconds** edits the battle opening, each phase's loop-in/out, phase-two downbeat and finishing passage. Edit the complete map, then **Apply cue map**; invalid loop ordering is rejected. The phase-two downbeat must leave four seconds for the pickup, and the finishing passage must leave seven seconds in the recording. The supplied map can be restored with **Reset cue map**. A new map/track deliberately restarts the corresponding section from the current cinematic/combat stage time; volume changes do not restart it. Source ranges are clamped/validated, and saved cursors identify their track so a different file cannot inherit an unrelated playback position.

Only one full battle buffer is active, with at most two briefly crossfading source voices. Importing a replacement can temporarily hold the old and newly decoded buffers. A 4:25 stereo recording uses roughly 89 MiB at 44.1 kHz, and longer imports cost proportionally more memory. The prelude continues to use streamed native media. Closing the encounter releases decoded playback buffers and media nodes; locally imported Blobs remain as user data for future sessions.
