# DCSMCRAFT 2.8.1 — Torches and persistent lighting

Placed torches use a narrow 2/16-block shaft, 10/16 blocks tall, with separate top, side and end UVs. Renewed and Soft use the opaque stem/flame pixels of its existing original sprite instead of shrinking an entire transparent sprite onto each face. Classic retains its existing atlas artwork and Supplied retains its existing cropped supplied texture. Item icons, held sprites, placement rules, recipes and block IDs are preserved.

Static block light is now derived from world blocks and baked into a separate vertex attribute. Every supported emitter in loaded detailed terrain contributes, independent of camera direction, player proximity, Fancy Graphics and the old four-point-light budget. Torches emit level 14; registered lamps, lava/flowing lava, fire, Emberglass and glow crystals emit their configured levels. Glow lichen and firefly bushes receive small appropriate light levels. Walls stop the flood, water attenuates it, and light crosses loaded chunk seams. Opening/closing a wall or adding/removing a lamp invalidates the affected neighboring meshes. A maximum 15-block halo fits the existing worker snapshot; lighting never requests distant chunks or scans the entire save history.

Dropped luminous block/items also contribute world-space light, whether or not their visual model passes the dropped-item distance or frustum checks. The derived positions update twice per second and coalesce items occupying the same cell, avoiding a remesh on every animation frame. Pickup/removal removes the source. Carried-item light follows the player and remains optional: Fancy Graphics plus **Carried Item Light: Off / Soft / Bright**. This replaces the misleading Nearby Block Lights budget label. Turning it off never extinguishes placed lamps.

Saves need no new envelope or block IDs: lighting rebuilds from the existing blocks/edits and saved dropped items. Chunk snapshots carry cached source positions; rebuilt source arrays and light attributes are disposable render data. Existing worlds require no regeneration.

## Limits

This is bounded voxel block lighting with a shared warm palette and stepped face values, not colored global illumination, ray tracing or a full Minecraft sky-light engine. Range is finite, like ordinary block light; blocks do not illuminate an infinite world. Fully unloaded chunks are not simulated for lighting. Distant heightfield proxies beyond detailed terrain still approximate world geometry and do not reproduce individual lamps or their flood field. Fog/render distance may hide distant geometry normally. Baked light covers voxel terrain surfaces; dynamic creatures/animated container models still use their ordinary scene illumination rather than sampling a complete per-entity light volume. Dropped-item light can take up to half a second plus the normal worker queue to update after movement/pickup.

## Verification

`tests/block-light.test.js` checks six simultaneous lamps, finite falloff, cross-chunk propagation, wall occlusion/reopening, water attenuation, snapshot reconstruction, torch dimensions, dropped emitters and pickup removal, the separate emissive shader attribute, and a dense 64-lamp budget. Existing streaming tests compare the actual bundled worker against authoritative main-thread snapshots. Browser checks use the final standalone and report pack UV opacity, camera/distance independence, actual GPU pixels, save/reload, dropped sources and any direct-file navigation limitations in VALIDATION.md. No claim of unlimited distant lighting or bug-free rendering is made.
