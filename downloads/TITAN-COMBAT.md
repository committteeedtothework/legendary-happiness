# DCSMCRAFT 2.8.5 — Giganto combat and cinematic polish

Changes are restricted to the singleplayer Titan encounter and its packaging/documentation. Portal discovery/entry, floor rings, the twelve-step final-Emerald ascent, Super transformation, two phases, supplied prelude/Undefeatable music, original-world snapshot, exactly-once rewards, retry and save envelope are retained. Ordinary sandbox graphics, combat, controls, content, music and multiplayer source/assets are unchanged, apart from the release label. Existing worlds require no regeneration. High-altitude checkpoints preserve the expanded flight range, including saved positions at Y129.

## Combat tuning

Normal Giganto retains **12,000 HP**; Easy 8,000, Hard 16,000. The new encounter health multiplier is 0.5–2.0 and applies on retry/new trial. Ring drain remains 1/sec before the existing Easy 0.65 / Normal 1 / Hard 1.3 factors. New trials still begin at 80 rings with a default 400 capacity and 36 persistent ten-ring trails; prepare before ascending. Direct hits now cost 9 / 14 / 20 rings and apply outward knockback with 1.25s invulnerability; failed struggles retain their 10 / 20 / 30 penalty. Protected cinematics and struggles pause ordinary ring drain. No artificial wait is added to fit the soundtrack.

Damage is applied at scheduled contacts, never at the initial key/button press. A four-hit chain deals 258 before Phantom Rush. Perfect parry requires guard to have been pressed within 0.22s of actual contact; ordinary held guard remains forgiving unless Strict Parry is enabled. Strict Parry accepts only the first 0.30s. A perfect parry restores 12 energy. Parry gives 1.8s stagger and a 2.4s counter opportunity with a 2.8s boss recovery; basic hits never stagger. Cyloop's 1.8s stagger has a separate 12s immunity timer and only applies on an actual nearby hit. Phantom Rush remains +20% damage for eight seconds at twelve connected hits, with a 16-second retrigger timer. Missing/damage breaks the relevant combo progression. One pending offensive action and consumed contact indices prevent repeated-input damage, overlapping specials or duplicated Rush hits. Dodge can cancel the pending action, forfeiting remaining contacts. Phase one clamps at 50% HP and clears pending contacts before phase two.

**Pause → Titan Signals** exposes every damage value independently, a health multiplier, ring capacity/drain, difficulty, strict parry, encounter shadows and post-processing. Damage values are validated to 1–3 times their defaults. Damage changes apply live; health changes require retry/new trial. Costs/timing/reach are data-driven in `src/titan-combat.js`. The automated best-effort control-input playthrough and duration are reported in VALIDATION.md; time depends on preparation, movement, accuracy and difficulty, and no universal duration is promised.

## Movement and contact

WASD uses the manually controlled camera yaw in both targeting modes; lock-on never overwrites yaw. Tab toggles target lock. Moving the mouse strongly sideways disengages it. Both modes can orbit, retreat, rise/fall, boost and dodge during attacks. Normal combat speed is 17m/s and boost 36m/s, with existing energy drain and acceleration. Space/Ctrl rise/fall; landing enables grounded movement and jumping. Q performs a directional dodge with 0.48s immunity and a 0.85s cooldown. Pressing F initiates real velocity-based Wild Rush flight; it never assigns a fixed destination. Basic attacks do not move the saved avatar. Cyloop draws an energy path instead of hijacking Sonic and the camera for an orbit.

Mesh-calibrated ellipsoids cover torso, hips and head; analytic capsules cover shoulders, upper arms, forearms/hands, thighs and shins. Shared arm paths drive both runtime IK and collision volumes. Relative swept samples handle fast avatar/limb crossings; each scheduled attack contact is consumed once. Basic reach is 6.5–7.5m including forgiveness; counter 16m, Cyloop 13m and ranged Boom 43m from the nearest surface. Unlocked attacks require the target in the forward hemisphere with limited forgiveness; lock-on aims at the nearest region. Distant actions can whiff and spend energy; they cannot damage or remotely cancel the boss. Target text reports the selected body region and surface distance.

The old arena-wide arm hit test is replaced by the moving forearm sweep. Windups remain 1.5s left / 1.2s right before difficulty scaling; active sweeps last 0.65 / 0.55s. An attack can connect once, with a visible moving warning, then has its recovery gap. Mouth beam aims at a captured position and can be evaded before contact; a missed beam remains visibly aimed there. Grab likewise uses its captured location, not a global proximity grab. QTEs keep their fresh-tap gating. Position locks are limited to intro, transformation, phase change, finale and the brief beam/grab struggles; pause freezes their clocks. Solid ellipsoid trunk/head and capsule leg collision replaces the old full-height cylinder, leaving space around and below limbs. Decorative solid geometry remains beyond traversal bounds.

## Presentation and scalability

The angular blue/cyan HUD has a delayed damage trail, phase banner, ring/critical warnings, actual combo/Phantom meter, three live cooldown/energy panels, target/distance feedback, boost/speed, contextual warnings and timed-input prompts. Boss warnings take priority over offensive move labels. Full/compact layouts and reduced-motion CSS are included. Normal Minecraft-style UI is restored on leaving the trial. No camera icon is added.

Attack clips are retimed to current durations, with shared arm targeting, grounded run/jump versus aerial/boost transitions, small presentation lunges, recovery blending and bounded boss recoil. Dash/attack trails, directional contact sparks, surface-normal impact rings, partial Cyloop arc, strength-scaled Foley, contact flashes, stage ground shockwaves and existing bounded debris react to actual events. Hit-stop is a capped **25–55ms pose hold only**: simulation, movement, camera and warnings keep updating. No full-screen motion blur obscures contacts. Entrance/phase cameras emphasize scale; transformation lifts Sonic above the head, travels outward and descends continuously to the combat start. The finale moves outward and around the body from the current hero position before approaching the front; it does not cut through the torso/head. Ground-level foot contacts slide Sonic horizontally instead of pushing him below the arena floor. Enter skips protected cinematics and clears pending attacks/QTE state, returning to controls without stale actions. Camera smoothing/body clearance remain active across shot changes.

Scenery adds weathered ruined colonnades/arches and distant mountains, with a wider, terraced transition into the basin rim. Instancing/merged geometry and existing quality-dependent foliage/effects remain. Directional character shadows have a correctly updated fixed frustum, bias and normal bias; no camera-following shadow frustum shimmers. Arena Shadows Auto follows the ordinary Shadows setting; Off disables them, Low uses 1024² and High 2048². Ground/scenery receive shadows and characters/ruins cast them. A cheap soft ground-contact disk fades with Sonic's altitude. Medium/High graphics optionally add the existing bloom/environment/output passes; encounter post-processing can be disabled independently. All renderer shadow/tone/exposure state is restored after every arena draw and on exit; maps, passes, textures and per-encounter resources are disposed.

## Honest limits

This is an original higher-detail stylized procedural PBR arena and a custom Frontiers-inspired encounter. It does not reproduce the commercial game's complete move/cinematic library or photorealistic scenery. Existing user-supplied meshes and 22 Sonic / 24 Giganto GLB clips remain; this release improves engine timing, posing, IK, transitions and presentation, without claiming new motion-captured clips. Individual finger/facial performance, direct mesh wall-running/climbing and mesh-exact grabbed-hand contacts are incomplete. Ellipsoids/capsules approximate visible anatomy, including interpolated elbows and small forgiveness, rather than per-triangle animated collision. Boom uses a timed ranged contact with effects rather than a fully simulated free projectile. The contact disk is an approximation, not screen-space ambient occlusion; directional shadows are single-frustum PCF, not cascades/ray tracing. Screenshot composition is inspired by Frontiers, using original vector/system-font UI. Model/audio attribution and existing soundtrack phase-cue limitations remain in TITAN-ASSETS.md, THIRD_PARTY.md and GIGANTO-MUSIC.md. Native Windows and direct file-opening test limitations are stated in VALIDATION.md.
